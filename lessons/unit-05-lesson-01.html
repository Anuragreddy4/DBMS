UNIT 05 · LESSON 01

Locks, 2PL & deadlocks

IN PLAIN WORDS

Locks control who may read or change an item at the same time. Two-phase locking gives a safe pattern; a wait-for graph helps find deadlocks.

By the end, you can:
  • Explain shared and exclusive locks
  • Trace basic and strict 2PL
  • Detect a deadlock in a wait-for graph
TOPICS IN THIS LESSON
01Shared and exclusive locks↘02Two-phase locking↘03Deadlock↘
EXPLANATION
TOPIC 01

Shared and exclusive locks

DEFINITION

A shared (S) lock allows reading. An exclusive (X) lock allows reading and writing and must be held alone.

Several transactions can hold S on the same item at once. An X request conflicts with S or X already held by another transaction.

For multiple-granularity locking, IS and IX announce plans to lock smaller objects; SIX combines shared access with an intention to take exclusive locks below.

QUICK EXAMPLE

Two users may both read a course description under S locks. A writer needs X before changing that description.

TOPIC 02

Two-phase locking

DEFINITION

2PL has a growing phase, when locks may be acquired, and a shrinking phase, when locks may be released but no new locks may be acquired.

Basic 2PL guarantees conflict serializability. Strict 2PL holds exclusive locks until commit or abort, preventing others from reading or overwriting those uncommitted writes.

Conservative 2PL asks for every needed lock before the transaction starts. It prevents deadlock but may make a transaction wait even when some of its requested items are free.

QUICK EXAMPLE

After T1 releases its first lock, it cannot acquire a new one under 2PL.

TOPIC 03

Deadlock

DEFINITION

A deadlock is a cycle of transactions waiting for locks held by one another.

In a wait-for graph, Ti→Tj means Ti is waiting for a lock held by Tj. A directed cycle means no transaction in the cycle can proceed without intervention.

A DBMS can prevent deadlock, detect a cycle and roll back a victim, or use a timeout.

QUICK EXAMPLE

If T1 holds A and waits for B, while T2 holds B and waits for A, the graph T1→T2→T1 is a deadlock.

WORKED EXAMPLE · SELECT A STEP

See why two transactions can deadlock

T1 needs to update records A and B. T2 also needs both records. A lock means one transaction has temporary control of a record.

TransactionHas a lock onWaiting for
T1AB, held by T2
T2BA, held by T1
STEP 01

Each gets one lock

T1 locks A. T2 locks B. Neither can change the other's locked record yet.

STEP 02

Each asks for the other record

T1 waits for B. T2 waits for A.

STEP 03

See the loop

T1 is waiting for T2, and T2 is waiting for T1. Neither can continue: this is a deadlock.

RESULT

The DBMS can break the deadlock by cancelling one transaction, which releases its lock so the other can finish.

EXAM FOCUS

What to be ready to explain

Show how 2PL ensures conflict serializability. Compare its variants, explain strict 2PL, state lock compatibility, and detect deadlock from a wait-for graph.
