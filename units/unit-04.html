LESSON 0101

Transactions, ACID & transaction states

A transaction is one complete job, such as moving money between accounts. ACID describes its promises; logs and recovery help make a committed job durable and an incomplete job atomic.

Open this lesson ↗
LESSON 0202

Schedules, concurrency & conflicts

A schedule is the order in which transactions' reads and writes happen. Interleaving can save time, but conflicting operations need a safe order.

Open this lesson ↗
LESSON 0303

Conflict & view serializability

Serializability asks whether an interleaved schedule gives the same result as some safe serial order. A precedence graph turns that question into a cycle check.

Open this lesson ↗
LESSON 0404

Recoverable, cascadeless & strict schedules

Recoverability is about commit order after a read. Cascadeless and strict schedules add stronger rules that make failures easier to handle.

LESSON 01

Transactions, ACID & transaction states

Open lesson ↗
01 TransactionA transaction is a sequence of database operations treated as one logical unit of work. ↗ 02 The four ACID promisesACID stands for Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, and Durability. ↗ 03 Transaction statesA transaction moves from active work toward commit, or from failure toward abort. ↗ 04 Implementation of atomicity and durabilityAtomicity means a transaction takes effect completely or not at all. Durability means a committed result survives a crash. ↗
LESSON 02

Schedules, concurrency & conflicts

Open lesson ↗
01 Serial and concurrent schedulesA schedule lists operations from one or more transactions. A serial schedule runs one whole transaction before another; a concurrent schedule interleaves their operations. ↗ 02 When do operations conflict?Two operations conflict if they are from different transactions, access the same item, and at least one is a write. ↗ 03 Problems caused by uncontrolled overlapConcurrency anomalies are incorrect results caused by unsafe interleaving. ↗
LESSON 03

Conflict & view serializability

Open lesson ↗
01 Conflict serializabilityA schedule is conflict-serializable if swapping adjacent non-conflicting operations can turn it into a serial schedule. ↗ 02 Cycles in the precedence graphA directed cycle means the transactions require contradictory orders, so the schedule is not conflict-serializable. ↗ 03 View serializabilityView equivalence preserves which transaction each read gets its value from and which transaction performs the final write. ↗ 04 Testing a schedule for serializabilityThe precedence-graph test decides conflict serializability by turning conflicting operation order into directed edges between transactions. ↗
LESSON 04

Recoverable, cascadeless & strict schedules

Open lesson ↗
01 Recoverable scheduleIf T2 reads a value written by T1, a recoverable schedule makes T2 commit only after T1 commits. ↗ 02 Cascadeless scheduleA cascadeless schedule allows a transaction to read a value only after the transaction that wrote it has committed. ↗ 03 Strict scheduleA strict schedule prevents other transactions from reading or writing an item that a transaction has written but not yet committed or aborted. ↗
